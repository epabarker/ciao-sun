# What does Protocols say about evening light, and do full texts falsify it?

Research for [#20](https://github.com/epabarker/daylight-viewing/issues/20) (map [#2](https://github.com/epabarker/daylight-viewing/issues/2)). It applies the standard of [#9](https://github.com/epabarker/daylight-viewing/issues/9) ([protocols-falsification.md](https://github.com/epabarker/daylight-viewing/blob/research/protocols-falsification/research/protocols-falsification.md)) to the book's evening-light claims, for the tool now being renamed **Ciao Sun** (formerly sunspot).

**Terms.** Terms follow `GLOSSARY.md`: **Morning light**, **Nominal sunrise**, **Candidate**, **Horizon profile**, **Clear window**, **Wake time**, **Morning window**, **Sun minutes**, **Sky openness**, **Duration target**, **Protocols factor**, **Stage**. New evening terms are marked *(proposal)* and collected at the end of the modelling section.

**Copyright.** The book is paraphrased throughout. No book text is reproduced. Claims are located by protocol name and endnote key only. SP = Sleep Protocol, LP = Light Protocol, SCP = Stress Control Protocol.

## The standard

As in #9, the book is the source of truth. A claim **stands unless full-text primary evidence falsifies it**.

- Speculative support counts if nothing contradicts it. That includes animal, retinal, modelling and mechanistic work.
- Being unproven, untested in humans or weakly cited is **not** a reason to downgrade a claim.
- A null result falsifies only when the study tested the claim directly (same population, timing, setting, stimulus and outcome) and had the power to detect the effect.
- Verdicts rest on full texts, not abstracts. Where a full text could not be obtained, the verdict is **not assessable**.

Verdicts:

- **Falsified**: a specific full-text result contradicts the claim.
- **Not falsified, supported**: full-text evidence is consistent with the claim, marked *strong* (replicated or direct measurement) or *speculative* (animal, anatomy, modelling, mechanism or indirect).
- **Not falsified, untested**: no full text tests the claim either way.
- **Not assessable**: the full text that would decide the claim could not be obtained.

## Where the book covers evening light

A search of every chapter and the endnotes for evening, sunset, afternoon, late-day, dusk, twilight and night-light content found four places that matter:

- **SP2** (Sleep Protocol 2, view sunlight in the late afternoon). The main evening protocol: timing, duration, geometry, the two-oscillator mechanism and the buffer against night light. Notes ch01en_64 to ch01en_69.
- **LP2** (Light Protocol 2, view late-afternoon or early-evening sunlight to buffer against artificial light at night). Duration, the "no lamp in the evening" rule, light quality at low sun, and the buffering studies. Notes ch05en_33 to ch05en_38.
- **LP3** (Light Protocol 3, limit bright light in the last hour before sleep). Its four-phase daily schedule ties the evening session to hours since waking. Note ch05en_39 onwards is about night light.
- **SP1** "Your Eyes" and the Chapter 5 "What Is Light?" section. These extend the sunrise colour-contrast claim to sunset. Notes ch01en_34, ch01en_35 and ch01en_40.

SP3, SP15 (jet lag), SCP5 and LP5 mention sunset or evening only in passing: night-light avoidance, travel timing, evening cortisol and low UV at low sun. They are not separate evening-sunlight claims, except where noted in section F.

## TL;DR

1. **1 of 22 evening sub-claims is falsified, and only in part.**
   - SP2 says sunlight from late afternoon into early evening delays the clock, and LP3 places the session 6–10 h after waking.
   - Four full-text phase-response curves (PRCs) agree that light starting about **6–8 h after waking is in the advance region**, not the delay region [6][7][8][9].
   - About 8–10 h after waking is a transition zone. There the curves disagree on where the sign flips and the fitted shifts are near zero, so it is **not falsified**.
   - From about 10–11 h after waking (early evening on a 07:00–23:00 day), light delays, as the book says [6][7][8][9]. Every other claim is not falsified: 16 are supported (5 of them only speculatively), and 5 are untested.
2. **The book's main reason for evening light holds.** That reason is the buffer against artificial light at night. Bright light earlier in the day or early evening reduces later light-induced melatonin suppression and phase delay [1][3][4][5]. The caveat: no study used *evening sunlight* itself. The evidence comes from lamps or from total daytime light.
3. **The adolescent caveat holds, but the book misdescribes the study.** The cited study used laboratory fluorescent light, not sunlight. Its bright afternoon-to-evening light lowered later melatonin and delayed melatonin onset by about 21 min in adolescents [2]. That is consistent with the book's "doesn't protect adolescents" point, but stronger than it. The book's account of the te Kulve groups is also wrong in its details; the main result stands [1].
4. **The book's own timing is internally inconsistent.** SP2 says late afternoon or early evening, before sunset. LP2 says any time after solar noon and before sunset. LP3 says 6–10 h after waking. In Bristol with a 07:00 Wake time:
   - in December, sunset (16:03) falls inside LP3's window;
   - in June, all low sun (below 30°, from 17:55) and sunset (21:31) fall in what LP3 calls the dim-the-lights phase.
5. **Sunset light quality: same verdict as sunrise in #9.** It is not falsified, with speculative support [13][15][16]. The low direct beam is long-wavelength-weighted on clear days [13]. The claim that this signals night is a hypothesis. The evening and night human tests fail the falsification bar [17][18].
6. **Geometry and practice mirror the morning.** Head or face towards the western sun. A view of the sun going down is welcome but optional; ambient daylight is enough. Stay outdoors, with no sunglasses or brim, and don't stare. Overcast days matter more. Don't substitute a 10,000 lux lamp in the evening. None of these is falsified.
7. **For the tool:**
   - Model an *Evening window (proposal)* anchored at its end to sunset, with sky openness to the west as the backbone.
   - Treat the disc in view as a bonus.
   - Extend the Protocols factor to the evening low sun.
   - Treat cloud as a multiplier, never a veto.
   - The Horizon profile already covers the western horizon, so no new data are needed.
   - The user must decide the anchor, the Duration target, and whether evening joins Stage 1.

**Noted for honesty (not a verdict).** The book's account of te Kulve 2019 [1] is wrong in its details:

- It describes three groups, the third getting bright early-evening light and then dim light late at night.
- The real study was a within-person crossover of 12 women, so there were no separate groups.
- The protective condition was bright light from 18:30 to 21:00 followed by **bright** (750 lx) late-evening light.
- The all-dim session was the reference, with no suppression, so it did not show "some reduction in melatonin" as the book says all groups did.

The book's summary of Hébert 2002 [3] is roughly right on the bright week (up to about five hours; the measured mean was 4.3 h, range 3.8–4.9). It is off on the dim week: outdoor time averaged 1.4 h, with a range up to 3.5 h, not "at most 2.5 h". The main conclusions the book draws from both studies match the full texts.

What it means:

- **Winter.**
  - The whole afternoon is low sun.
  - Sunset falls inside LP3's window, so every version of the book's advice agrees.
  - Sunset comes about 9 h after waking. Light from 13:00 to about 15:00 (6–8 h after waking) is in the PRC advance region, and the last hour before sunset is in the transition zone (B2b). So winter evening light *adds to*, or is neutral to, the morning advance rather than offsetting it.
- **Summer.**
  - LP3's 13:00–17:00 has the sun at 30–62°.
  - All low sun (0–30°) and sunset fall in Phase 3 or 4, which LP3 reserves for dim light, and in the PRC delay region (B2a).
  - SP2 and LP2 (before sunset) and LP3 (6–10 h after waking) give different answers.

## What the tool should model (book stands unless falsified)

### 1. Evening window anchor: sunset or schedule?

The book offers three anchors (A1, A2). Under the standard, all three stand, so this is the user's choice.

- **Option A, solar (LP2):** from solar noon to Nominal sunset, or to Last sun at the Candidate. This is the simplest and needs no schedule input.
- **Option B, schedule (LP3):** from Wake time + 6 h to Wake time + 10 h, clipped to sunset. It needs only Wake time, which is already a setting. In a Bristol summer it would never include low sun.
- **Option C, hybrid (suggested):** from the later of solar noon and Wake time + 6 h, to Last sun at the Candidate. Show a one-line note when the window runs past Wake time + 10 h (LP3's dim-the-lights phase).

Under every option:

- **No first-hour weighting.** The book gives the evening no priority hour; the cortisol window is morning-only. Count total minutes, as in the morning. Let the Protocols factor (section 3) favour the low sun.
- **Stop at Last sun.** The book says before sunset. Twilight after sunset is bluer [15] and is not part of any protocol, so it gets no credit.
- **Don't build the window on the "delay" mechanism.** B2b is falsified for the first part of LP3's window (6–8 h after waking). The window's value should rest on the buffer (D1, D3) and the book's light-quality claim (C1–C3), neither of which depends on hitting the delay zone.

### 2. Duration target (evening)

The book gives at least 10–15 min (SP2) and 5–30 min (LP2), with no cloud scaling, although LP2 says to follow LP1's general guidelines. Options:

- **(i)** Reuse the morning's 10/20/30 min for clear, partly cloudy and overcast. This reads LP2's pointer to LP1 as importing the cloud scaling. It sits inside LP2's 5–30 and meets SP2's 10–15 floor for clear skies.
- **(ii)** A flat 10–15 min whatever the cloud.

Either way:

- Count total outdoor minutes, including the walk.
- Show it as a target to aim for, not a threshold, in line with the glossary.
- Flag the SP2/LP2 inconsistency, as #9 did for the morning.

### 3. Solar angle, disc in view, open sky

The structure mirrors #9's morning model.

- **Backbone: Sky openness, weighted to the western sector.** Use the afternoon part of that day's sun path, about 180° to 232° in winter and to 311° in summer in Bristol. Ambient light is enough, so this is the dose (E2) [19].
- **Clear window or disc in view: a ranked bonus, not a gate.** The book says face west and treats seeing the sun set as a bonus (E2).
- **Protocols factor at the evening low sun.** Give the bonus when the sun is visible above the Horizon profile, its elevation is about 0–30°, and the sky is forecast clear. That is the only regime where the yellow-shifted direct beam exists [13]. Label it, as in the morning, as untested in humans (C1–C3). Never penalise low elevation.
- **The same horizon rules apply at both ends of the day.** Last sun uses the disc-centre-above-profile rule, including profiles below 0° ([#14](https://github.com/epabarker/daylight-viewing/issues/14)), and the far-field merge to 100 km ([#13](https://github.com/epabarker/daylight-viewing/issues/13)). In Bristol, 3 km made morning leave-by times 3–5 min early from late September to February. Expect the same order of error for winter Last sun to the south-west, so keep the far field.

### 4. Cloud

- **Cloud is a multiplier, never a veto.** The book says overcast days matter *more* (E4), so never suppress the evening suggestion under cloud. Cloud scales the dose by about 10^(−0.08 × oktas) [13].
- **Use an evening cloud category.** Compute the three-level category as mean cover over the Evening window. #11 currently defines it over the Morning window, so it needs an evening twin. Open-Meteo's roughly 2-day hourly horizon covers today's and tomorrow's evenings.
- The Protocols factor needs a clear forecast, because cloud removes the direct beam and shifts low-sun light bluer [13].

### 5. How evening interacts with morning

- **Complementary, not a replacement.** The book makes morning sunlight the foundation (SP1) and evening an addition (SP2, LP2). Evening has its own value, the buffer against night light (D1), which is largest when mornings and days are spent indoors (D3).
- **Most useful on winter days.** In Bristol in winter, sunrise can fall more than an hour after Wake time, so the outdoor cortisol hour is unavailable (#9 implication 5). On those days evening light is the book's remaining outdoor light session, and the tool could surface it.
- **Phase effects change with the season.**
  - Winter evening light falls in the advance region or the transition zone, so it reinforces the morning or is neutral.
  - Summer low sun falls in the delay region, which is the "offset" the book describes.
  - A natural full day plus darkness after sunset still produced an earlier phase, not a later one [12]. What undoes morning gains is light *after* dark [23], not sunset.
- **No lamp fallback in the evening (E5).** The morning 10,000 lux fallback stays morning-only.
- **One line of copy is enough.** Example: after sunset, dim the lights (LP3, SP3).

### 6. Glossary terms the user will need

All of these are proposals:

- **Evening light** *(proposal)*: the late-day sunlight exposure, after solar noon and before sunset, that the tool helps someone get. It is the counterpart to Morning light. *Avoid*: sunset hit, golden hour.
- **Nominal sunset** *(proposal)*: the last minute the sun's geometric centre is at or above −0.833° at a location. It mirrors Nominal sunrise and depends only on place and date.
- **Last sun** *(proposal)*: the last minute that day the sun's disc centre is above a Candidate's Horizon profile. It can be well before Nominal sunset behind obstructions. It replaces "leave-by" in the evening timeline, becoming "arrive by".
- **Evening window** *(proposal)*: the period in which Evening light counts. Its anchor is open (section 1), and it is not weighted to a first hour.
- **Light window** *(proposal, optional)*: a parent term for the Morning window and the Evening window, so that Sun minutes, Duration target and Protocols factor can be defined once.
- **Existing terms to generalise.** Sun minutes and Duration target currently say "within the Morning window" or "on a given morning". The Protocols factor currently says "when the sun is low" without saying which end of the day. Wake time currently anchors only the Morning window; under option B or C it anchors the Evening window too.
- **Glossary title.** It is still "sunspot"; rename it to Ciao Sun.

### 7. Interactions with existing decisions on #2

- **Horizon profile ([#8](https://github.com/epabarker/daylight-viewing/issues/8)).** It is date-independent and covers the full true azimuth at 0.25°, so the western horizon is already precomputed. Evening needs no new tiles and no new precompute.
- **Sky openness along that day's sun path ([#7](https://github.com/epabarker/daylight-viewing/issues/7)).** The same function evaluated on the afternoon part of the path gives the evening filter. The 0.75 cut-off ([#10](https://github.com/epabarker/daylight-viewing/issues/10)) can be reused, or set separately.
- **Ranking by low-sun minutes at 0–30°, taper-weighted ([#10](https://github.com/epabarker/daylight-viewing/issues/10)).** This works for the evening, but the morning's taper is weighted to the first hour after waking, which has no evening equivalent. In the evening, rank by low-sun minutes before Last sun without a wake-based taper. In a Bristol winter the whole afternoon is below 30°, so ranking will saturate even more than in the morning, and nearest-first will decide.
- **Protocols factor ([#9](https://github.com/epabarker/daylight-viewing/issues/9)).** It generalises to the evening low sun on the same evidence (C1), with the same "untested in humans" label.
- **Cloud source ([#11](https://github.com/epabarker/daylight-viewing/issues/11)).** It needs an Evening-window mean.
- **Far field ([#13](https://github.com/epabarker/daylight-viewing/issues/13)) and below-0° rule ([#14](https://github.com/epabarker/daylight-viewing/issues/14)).** They apply symmetrically to Last sun.
- **Max walk of 20 min, Bristol coverage, JS canonical scoring ([#8](https://github.com/epabarker/daylight-viewing/issues/8)).** Unchanged. Evening is another evaluation of the same tiles, at about the same cost per query.
- **Answer screen ([#10](https://github.com/epabarker/daylight-viewing/issues/10)).** The editable sentence would need a morning or evening switch, or both. The timeline bar becomes walk, sun, low sun and arrive-by or Last sun.

## Open questions for the user

1. **Evening window anchor.** Option A (solar), B (wake + 6–10 h) or C (hybrid). Also, should the window be cut off at Wake time + 10 h, which in a Bristol summer removes all evening low sun and the sunset itself?
2. **Evening Duration target.** Option (i), 10/20/30 by cloud, reading LP2's "same guidelines as LP1", or option (ii), a flat 10–15 min.
3. **Does evening join Stage 1?** And does it come as a separate mode, or as a second block on the same answer screen? The data and compute cost is near zero (section 7). The spec cost is the anchor, the targets, an evening cloud category and the timeline wording.
4. **Summer evening Protocols factor.** Keep it even though the low sun falls in LP3's dim-the-lights phase, or suppress it after Wake time + 10 h?
5. **Copy about B2b.** Should the tool say that afternoon light starting about 6–8 h after waking shifts the clock earlier, not later? This is the one partial falsification, and it means winter evening light reinforces the morning.

## Method note

- The research was done inline by the delegated agent, without a further background agent.
- The book was extracted from the user's EPUB into a fresh scratch directory and converted to text. All chapters and the three endnote files were searched for evening, sunset, afternoon, late-day, dusk, twilight, sundown, after-dark, west, protect and buffer.
- Full texts came from PubMed Central through the PubMed tool unless marked otherwise.
- Papers marked "via fetch summary" were read on their PMC article page with a fetch tool that summarises pages. Specific numbers and sentences were requested and checked against a second targeted fetch where they decide a verdict (Revell 2012's afternoon statements).
- Solar times come from a NOAA-style approximation, not pvlib, and are good to about ±2 min.
